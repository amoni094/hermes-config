## Key Terms Dictionary

**Anagoge** — Plato's term for the upward ascent through levels of reality toward the Form of the Good; simultaneously epistemic (knowing better) and existential (becoming better).

**Attentional scaling (up/down)** — Two modes of mindfulness attention: *scaling down* = breaking gestalt into features, de-automatizing, transparency-opacity shift (meditation/Vipassana); *scaling up* = integrating features into gestalt, looking more deeply into reality (contemplation/Metta).

**Axial Age / Axial Revolution** — ~800–300 BCE period (Jaspers) when Greek philosophy, Hebrew prophecy, Indian Buddhism, and Chinese thought independently awoke to interiority, second-order thinking, and the capacity for self-transcendence and self-correction.

**Character** (Aristotelian) — Not personality (given by biology/environment) but the cultivated set of virtues that constitute one's *virtual engine* — the constraint system regulating self-development.

**Conformity theory of knowledge** — Aristotle: to know something is to have one's mind take on the same structural-functional organization (*idos*) as the thing; knowledge is participatory contact, not mere description.

**Continuous cosmos** — Pre-Axial worldview in which human, natural, and divine realms interpenetrate without sharp ontological boundaries; shattered by the Two-Worlds picture and then the Scientific Revolution.

**Depth landscape** — Third level of Vervaeke's consciousness model: tracking causal (not merely correlational) patterns through active interaction with the world.

**Dynamical systems theory / Virtual engine** — Alicia Juarrero's Aristotle-inspired framework: self-organizing processes governed by enabling constraints (opening options) and selective constraints (closing options) in a feedback cycle; *virtual engine* = these two working together systematically.

**Existential mode** — The co-identification of Agent and Arena that constitutes a meta-meaning framework; all individual actions take their meaning from within a functioning existential mode.

**Exaptation** — Co-opting a cognitive/biological mechanism evolved for one purpose to serve another (e.g. tongue → speech; trading-ritual mindreading → shamanic insight).

**Flow state** (Csikszentmihalyi) — Optimal experience when task demands slightly exceed skills; characterized by insight cascade, at-onement, effortlessness, loss of self-consciousness. More related to meaning in life than to pleasure.

**Idos** (Aristotle/Plato) — Structural-functional organization of a thing; what makes a bird a bird; the pattern by which we come to know it (NOT just shape/appearance).

**Implicit learning** (Reber) — Unconscious pickup of complex environmental patterns; "intuition" when it tracks causal patterns, "bias/prejudice" when it tracks only correlational patterns.

**Modal confusion** (Fromm) — Trying to satisfy *being needs* (maturity, love, meaning) through *having strategies* (buying things, accumulating status); the existential pathology illustrated by Siddhartha's palace.

**Nomological order** — Worldview in which scientific understanding and existential dwelling mutually support each other, reliably generating functioning existential modes; its breakdown produces absurdity.

**Non-duality (prajna)** — The third and target mystical state in Vervaeke's model: integration of pure consciousness event (scaled-down) and resonant at-onement (scaled-up); produces comprehensive insight of consciousness, not just a point insight.

**Ontonormativity** — The puzzling feature of higher states of consciousness: they feel *more real* and normatively demand transformation despite being temporary, ineffable, and incoherent with everyday experience.

**Optimal grip** (Merleau-Ponty tradition) — Dynamic balance between gestalt and feature in perception/action; neither too close (lose gestalt) nor too far (lose detail).

**Participatory knowing** — Knowing by conforming to, enacting, or identifying with something (vs. propositional knowing = having beliefs about it; procedural knowing = knowing how; perspectival knowing = knowing what it is like).

**Presence landscape** — Second level of consciousness model: the affordance network generated through agent-arena co-identification and optimal gripping.

**Procedural knowing** — Knowing *how* to do something (catch a baseball, speak a language); distinct from propositional knowing (beliefs).

**Propositional knowing** — Knowing *that* something is the case; justified true belief; the impoverished modern default.

**Psycho-technology** — A standardized cognitive practice (literacy, numeracy, meditation, ritual, coinage) that fits the brain as a tool fits the body, enhancing cognitive capacity by linking brain states across time or across individuals.

**Pure consciousness event (PCE)** (Forman) — Mystical state produced by maximal scaling-down: conscious but not conscious *of* anything; pure presence without content.

**Quantum change theory** — Empirical observation that some people undergo radical, comprehensive life transformation after higher states of consciousness; verified by surveys showing significant meaning-in-life improvement.

**Relevance realization** — The core function of consciousness: dynamically selecting, foregrounding, and configuring what is relevant from the infinite available information; failure = meaning crisis.

**Resonant at-onement** — Mystical state produced by maximal scaling-up: comprehensive participation and flowing identity with everything; super-flow state.

**Salience landscape** — First level of consciousness model: the dynamic texture of what stands out, gets foregrounded, and becomes figured in experience; altered states transform it.

**Sati** — Pali: often translated "mindfulness" but Vervaeke argues the correct translation is *remembering/recovering the being mode*; a modal memory, not attentional focus on the present moment.

**Second-order thinking** (Bellah) — Internalizing a psycho-technology (especially alphabetic literacy) into metacognition, enabling reflective self-correction; the cognitive engine of the Axial Revolution.

**The Two-Worlds picture** — Platonic legacy: a higher, more-real world (Forms/God) vs. the lower apparent world; humans caught between. Becomes the template for Christian theology and eventually the modern subject/object split.

**Worldview attunement** (Geertz/Aristotle) — The ongoing consonance between one's cosmological picture and one's existential mode; Aristotle's geocentric cosmos + conformity theory mutually reinforce this.

## Series Topology

**The diagnostic arc (Eps 1–7):** Establishes how humans evolved meaning-making (shamanism, flow, metaphor), how the Axial Revolution transformed it (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle), and how Aristotle's worldview attunement created the nomological order that was later shattered.

**The Buddhist turn (Eps 7–9):** Pivots to India: modal confusion (Fromm), Siddhartha's myth as its diagnosis and cure, mindfulness as a set of psycho-technologies for recovering the being mode, and insight/awakening as the goal.

**The cognitive-scientific foundation (Eps 9–10):** Grounds everything in attention theory (Polanyi, Mole), mystical state taxonomy (PCE / resonant at-onement / non-duality), and consciousness as relevance realization (global workspace, IIT, salience/presence/depth landscapes).

**The implicit through-line:** The meaning crisis = the collapse of nomological order (worldview attunement) combined with loss of participatory/perspectival/procedural knowing, leaving only propositional knowing. Recovery = re-cultivating psycho-technologies (mindfulness, transformative experience) that upgrade relevance realization and restore being-mode access.
